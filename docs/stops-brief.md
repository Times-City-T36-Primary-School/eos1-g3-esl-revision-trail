# The nine stops: what each one covers (Grade 3, Cambridge Primary ESL Stage 4)

All stops use the four Semester 1 unit topics as the setting, spread evenly: 1 Our Community; 2 Earth and Beyond; 3 Homes; 4 Food (sub-topics and vocabulary in `exam-style-guide-esl.md`).
Level: children aged 8 to 9, about CEFR A1+ to A2. Short, simple sentences (most under 12 words), one idea per sentence. Much shorter and simpler than Grade 5. Follow the lengths in the style guide.
Every multiple-choice question in the Grade 3 exam has exactly three options, and the Reading and Use of English paper has NO typed answers: use mcq for all Reading and Use of English exam-style items. Typed answers (tfill) appear only in Listening Part 3 (the form) and in some Learn it rounds.

## Per-stop notes
- Stops 1 to 3: "Learn it" = one-step questions on the rule (mcq, and some tfill with one word). "Exam style" = items in the format of Reading Part 3 (one sentence of 6 to 14 words with a gap "________" and 3 words as options; mcq), Part 2 (a spoken line in quotation marks in q, "Choose the best reply", three replies; mcq) and Part 4 (one gap of a short recount in the stim, gaps numbered, three options for the gap; mcq). Lightning = quick grammar or word choices.
- Stop 4: every listening item has an audio stim. Learn it: Level 1 numbers, times, prices, days (pmcq with tags); Level 2 traps: "No, ...", "usually ... but today", a changed mind (pmcq); Level 3 short form lines: a speaker spells a name letter by letter, says a phone number digit by digit, an age, a time (tfill from short talks, 1 or 2 gaps). Exam style: Level 1 and 2 = Part 1 (pmcq, a child and an adult, 10 to 16 s per exchange; a set of exchanges can share the same two characters); Level 3 = Part 3 (one conversation of about 65 s: an adult asks a child questions in form order; 3 rounds = 3 gaps of the SAME talk; the second variant uses a second talk). In every form at least one answer is spelled letter by letter (write it in the script as letters with hyphens: "It's G-R-A-Y.") and one may be a phone number (accept it with and without spaces). Test: items 1 to 5 (DOK 1) = the 5 gaps of ONE Part 3 conversation (v[1] of each uses a second conversation); items 6 to 10 (DOK 2) = 5 Part 1 pmcq where the child must rule options out (a correction, a changed plan).
- Stop 5: every item has an audio stim. Each item = ONE short conversation between two children (23 to 30 s; child voices bf_lily / bf_alice and bm_lewis / bm_fable), the context line in q ("You will hear two friends talking about a new game. What does the girl like best?"), three short written options, all three heard in the talk. Questions ask about feelings, opinions, reasons, preferences, what someone will do. Learn it: shorter exchanges (15 to 20 s). Exam style: 3 levels x 3 rounds x 2 variants, each a separate conversation. Test: 10 separate conversations; DOK 1 = said directly, DOK 2 = opinion, reason, paraphrase.
- Stop 6: Learn it: Level 1 sign words (must, mustn't, don't, only, closed, open, please, free); Level 2 short messages (who wrote it, what to do); Level 3 replies (what answers a question, an offer, thanks, bad news). Exam style: Part 1 = a sign, notice, message or a small table (an HTML table) in the stim (2 to 20 words; "head" in capitals; you may put one emoji picture in the html), q "What does it say?" or a specific question, three sentence options that paraphrase; Part 2 = a spoken line in q in quotation marks, three possible replies, only one fits (wrong ones answer a different question, use the wrong tense, or agree when they should disagree). Test: items 1 to 5 = Part 1 (DOK 1 and direct), items 6 to 10 = Part 2 (DOK 2).
- Stop 7: Exam style Part 3 = one sentence with a gap and three words (three forms or three similar words); Part 4 = a recount (about 150 to 180 words, title, first person, past simple) in the stim with 7 numbered gaps; each item is ONE gap: q "Gap (3): choose the word.", three options (e.g. "a few / a little / much"). Test: items 1 to 5 = Part 3 (DOK 1), items 6 to 10 = Part 4 gaps from ONE recount (DOK 2).
- Stop 8: Part 5 = a stim with THREE first-person texts by three children (about 65 words each, same theme, different home, hobby or experience), and mcq "Who ...?" questions whose 3 options are the three children's names (opts[0] the right name). A name may be the answer more than once. Questions paraphrase; the other two texts mention something similar. Part 6 = a third-person article about a child or a class (about 200 words, title, one short quotation from a person) with mcq questions in text order (why something happened, how someone felt, what surprised someone, what someone will do next, and sometimes the main idea). Test: items 1 to 5 = Part 6 (some DOK 1), items 6 to 10 = Part 5 (DOK 2).
- Stop 9 (Writing): see "Writing stop" below.

(The cousin task above is only a format example: do NOT use it.)
Write 3 Task 1 tasks (describe a PERSON: who, what they are like, what they do, why they matter to you) and 3 Task 2 tasks (describe a PLACE or THING linked to a unit topic: what and where, what it looks like, one special feature and what it is used for (to + verb), why you like it). min 40, max 70, needTitle false. Each task: 4 bullets as questions, "Use describing words and linking words." in the prompt, and 3 models: Developing (a little short, 30 to 38 words, 2 or 3 typical learner errors: missing capital letters or full stops, a wrong verb form, a spelling slip), Secure and Excellent (inside 40 to 70 words, one paragraph). Each model has "marks" with EXACTLY these keys, values 0 to 2: content, organisation, grammar, vocabulary, conventions. The rubric (the school's real Grade 3 categories, 2 marks each, 10 per task):
- content = Task achievement and content (all four bullets, a reason for the opinion)
- organisation = Organisation and layout (one paragraph, logical order, linking words)
- grammar = Grammar range and accuracy (present simple, describing structures, to + verb for a use)
- vocabulary = Vocabulary and spelling (varied topic words, familiar words spelt right)
- conventions = Writing conventions (complete sentences, capital letters and full stops)
Notes: one per category in that order, each starting with the category name and the mark, e.g. "Writing conventions 2/2: ...". Totals: Developing about 4 to 5/10, Secure about 7 to 8/10, Excellent 10/10.
RESERVED for Stop 9 (and the Summit): Task 1 must NOT be about a person who helps you at school; Task 2 must NOT be about your dream home. Also not your best friend or a park / playground (Summit).
Test (10 items): mcq / tfill on the same writing skills (5 DOK 1, then 5 DOK 2).
