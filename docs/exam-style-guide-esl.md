# Exam-style guide: Grade 3 ESL (Cambridge Primary ESL Stage 4), End of Semester 1

This guide says HOW the Grade 3 ESL EOS1 exam asks questions. It never says WHAT it asks. Everything you write
must be new: new texts, new talks, new people, new places, new situations. Before you start, read the
"Reserved situations" list in `docs/stops-brief.md`: those situations must never appear anywhere in the trail.

## The exam at a glance

| Paper | Name on the paper | Minutes | Parts | Questions | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Listening | 25 | 3 | 15 | 15 |
| 2 | Reading and Use of English (the cover says "Reading"; the answer key says "Reading and Use of English") | 35 | 6 | 34 | 34 |
| 3 | Writing | 35 | 2 tasks | 2 | 20 (10 + 10) |
| Total (written papers) | | 95 | | 51 | 69 |

Speaking (Paper 4, 5 to 10 minutes per student) is a separate teacher-led test and is not part of the trail.
Every objective question is worth 1 mark. Students circle a letter, tick a box under a picture, circle a word, or write a word or number. There is ONE example at the start of most parts (marked 0 or "Example"), already answered.

## Level and language
- Learners: Vietnamese children aged 8 to 9, Cambridge Primary ESL Stage 4. Level about CEFR A1+ to A2 (Lexile 540L to 750L).
- British English spelling and vocabulary (colour, favourite, centre, practise (verb), programme, mum, flat, supper, football, holiday, autumn, timetable, film).
- Short, simple sentences (most under 12 words). One idea per sentence. Very concrete, everyday topics a young child can picture: home, family, school, friends, food, pets, the weather, the sky at night, the park, the garden, the neighbourhood. No brands, no real celebrities, no real living people.
- Vocabulary: high-frequency words plus the unit words listed below. Avoid idioms and phrasal verbs that are not taught at Stage 4.
- Names: use a mix of international names (e.g. Leo, Kenji, Priya, Sami, Isla, Tariq, Elena, Omar, Zara, Hugo, Nina, Max) and Vietnamese names (e.g. Minh, Lan, Huy, Mai, An, Bao, Linh, Nam, Vy, Khoa). Do not reuse a name inside the same stop for different people. The checker will reject names that clash with the real paper, so stick to these or ask.
- Thinking levels (Webb DOK). Level 1 = find or recognise something said or written directly. Level 2 = understand a paraphrase, infer, compare, decide what someone means, thinks or prefers. Level 3 is NOT used. The school's specification asks for at least 40% Level 1 and at least 20% Level 2 in Listening, Reading and Use of English. In the real paper about 55% of the objective items are Level 1 and 45% Level 2. In every set of 10 trail test items we use 5 + 5.

## The four EOS1 units (topics for all texts, talks and tasks)
1. Our Community: why families are special; neighbourhood, block, sports club, country, continent; what people are into or keen on; an inspiring (invented) sports player; sports words (long jump, skateboarding, rugby, karate, surfing); the school community and school jobs (cook, head teacher, class teacher, caretaker, school nurse, librarian, PE teacher); organising a community event (leaflet, raising money, craft activities); a treasure hunt in the neighbourhood (slide, swings, sandpit, surgery).
2. Earth and Beyond: describing planet Earth and weather (freezing, icy, dry, huge, wide, wet, humid, cool, warm); planets and orbits (explore, discover, poisonous, the eight planets); natural wonders (lightning, sunset, northern lights, constellation, rainbow); space technology (satellite, telescope, space shuttle, rover); a poem or story about a small planet.
3. Homes: describing where we live (balcony, floor, roof, chimney; bricks, wood, wool, canvas); types of home (detached house, stilt house, yurt, bungalow, flat); eco homes (solar panels, water tank, tubs to catch rain, recycling, turning off lights, unplugging chargers, collecting water); strange and famous buildings (library, hotel, museum, music academy, amusement park, sports centre); mountains and treks; a story home with round doors, tunnels and tiled floors.
4. Food: breakfasts around the world (papaya, nutritious, growing food); all about chocolate (cacao beans, pods, roast, crush, paste, vanilla); growing your own food (soil, seeds, ground, okra, bok choy, mango, cress, yoghurt pot, cotton wool, rinse, press, fold); a fruit poem (spiky, smooth, sweet, salty, juicy, smells like, tastes like); a chocolate factory story.

Unit vocabulary is free to use. What is reserved is the real paper's particular situations (see `stops-brief.md`), not the topic words themselves: any unit word may be used, but never inside a reserved situation.

## Use of English objectives tested (Stage 4)
- 4Ug.01 tag questions to seek agreement or check: It's cold, isn't it? / You've got a pet, haven't you? / She can swim, can't she? / They don't like rice, do they?
- 4Ug.03 imperatives, positive and negative, in a short sequence: Open the box. Don't touch the plant. Then wash your hands.
- 4Ug.10 have (got) to / had to for obligation; might, may, could for possibility: You have to wear a hat. We had to wait. It might snow. We could go tomorrow.
- 4Ug.11 adjectives, comparative and superlative (regular and irregular): big, bigger, the biggest; good, better, the best; bad, worse, the worst; more beautiful.
- 4Us.01 quantifiers: each, every, a few, few, a little, little, a lot of, much, many (with countable vs uncountable nouns).
- 4Us.02 indefinite pronouns: some, any, something, anything, nothing (I need something. There isn't anything. There's nothing in the box.).
- 4Us.03 connectives of time and sequence: when, before, after, then, while (Wash your hands before you eat. When I finish, I'll call you.).
- 4Us.06 verbs followed by infinitive or gerund: want / hope / learn / would like + to do; enjoy / love / like / finish + doing.
- 4Us.07 infinitive of purpose: I went to the shop to buy milk. We use the tub to catch rain.
- 4Uv.03 adverbs of indefinite time: yet, ever, already, always, never, hardly ever (I haven't finished yet. I've already eaten.).
- 4Uv.04 comparative and superlative adverbs: carefully, more carefully, most carefully; fast, faster; well, better, best.
- 4Uv.05 countable and uncountable nouns: an apple, some apples, some rice, a glass of milk, a few eggs, a little sugar.

What the real paper tests most: almost every objective appears TWICE, once in Reading Part 2 or 3 and once in the Part 4 text. The most frequent are quantifiers with countable / uncountable nouns (4Us.01 with 4Uv.05), indefinite pronouns (4Us.02), connectives (4Us.03), infinitive of purpose (4Us.07), might / may / have to (4Ug.10), comparative adverbs (4Uv.04), adverbs of indefinite time, especially already / yet (4Uv.03), tag questions (4Ug.01, two items) and verb + gerund / infinitive (4Us.06). Imperatives (4Ug.03) and comparative adjectives (4Ug.11) are tested mostly through context (inside replies, short texts and tables) rather than as a gap.

## Paper 1: Listening (25 minutes, 15 marks, 3 parts, 15 questions)
Every recording is heard TWICE. Each part starts with an example. The total recorded audio, with repeats and pauses, is about 14 to 15 minutes (the specification asks for 15 to 20 minutes including instructions). The talks are slow and clear, with pauses of about 3 to 5 seconds between questions. Spec talk lengths: short under 20 s, medium 20 to 49 s, long 50 to 70 s.

### Part 1 (questions 1 to 5, 5 marks): pictures A B C, tick a box
- Printed: a short question for each item (Where's ...? What's ... like? What does ... do? What is ... going to have ...? What time is ... going to ...?) and three black-and-white line pictures, A B C, each with a tick box. Students tick ONE box.
- Talk: ONE conversation between a child and an adult (for example an adult asking a child about their news), cut into five short exchanges, one per question, with the question read aloud before each exchange. All five questions are about the SAME child and their life. Each exchange is SHORT: 10 to 16 seconds, two speakers, 3 to 6 turns.
- Kinds of detail asked: a place, what a room or object looks like, a person's job, food or a meal, a clock time.
- Traps: all three pictures are mentioned or suggested. The adult guesses wrong and the child says no, then gives the right answer; the child says what they do NOT have before what they do have; two times are mentioned (what usually happens and what will happen today), and the right time follows from a reason the child gives. Pictures differ by only one or two details.
- DOK: about 4 at Level 1 (the answer is said after a "No"), 1 at Level 2 (a time worked out from "usually ... but today ...").

### Part 2 (questions 6 to 10, 5 marks): five conversations, three written options
- Printed: for each item, one context line ("You will hear two friends talking about ...", "You will hear a boy and his mum talking about ...") and a question, then three SHORT written options A B C (2 to 8 words: a noun phrase or a short sentence). Students circle A, B or C.
- Talk: five separate conversations, each 23 to 30 seconds (medium talk), two children (boy and girl), each played twice straight away ("Now listen again").
- Question styles: what someone liked most; why someone is doing something (a reason); what two speakers agree about; which one someone has chosen; what someone is interested in.
- Traps: ALL three options are mentioned in every conversation. The wrong ones are: something the speaker liked less or did not like; the OTHER speaker's wrong guess about the reason; what one speaker says but the other disagrees with; the first choice before the speaker changes their mind after a friend's comment; something that belongs to a third person; what the speaker did before or is already doing. Write your own signal phrases for the right answer (for example: the best part was ..., I don't agree ..., you might be right ..., I want to try something new ...).
- DOK: all 5 at Level 2 (opinion, reason, agreement, change of mind, preference).

### Part 3 (questions 11 to 15, 5 marks): complete a form
- Printed: an instruction naming the child and the situation (a child giving details to an adult to sign up for something), a form with a title in capitals, one example line already filled in (a name spelled in the recording), and five labelled gaps on dotted lines. Some gaps have printed words around them, so the answer is only the missing part.
- Talk: ONE conversation, about 65 seconds (long talk), an adult asking a child questions in the order of the form. Two speakers. Heard twice.
- Answers: one word, a number, or a number + word. Typical answer kinds: a number, a word that the child SPELLS letter by letter (must be spelt correctly to get the mark), a phone number given digit by digit, a length of time, a time or day.
- Traps: a second number close to the answer; a question that the child first misunderstands, so the adult asks it again in simpler words, or the child checks what the adult means before answering.
- DOK: all 5 at Level 1 (specific detail said directly).

## Paper 2: Reading and Use of English (35 minutes, 34 marks, 6 parts, 34 questions)
Total reading load in the real paper is about 900 words including questions; the specification target is 400 to 550 words of text, short texts under 30 words, medium 70 to 90, long 100 to 150 (the real Part 4 and Part 6 texts are longer, about 180 and 200 words). Use of English stems: short under 15 words, medium 15 to 30.

### Part 1 (questions 1 to 5, 5 marks): signs and notices
- Printed: five short real-life texts, each in a box with a heading in capitals and a simple picture: signs, notices, short messages, and one small table of results or information. Texts are 2 to 20 words. Next to each, a question and three options A B C (sentences of 6 to 10 words). The example is a very short sign with a "Who ...?" question.
- Question styles: what the notice says, what the notice means, why something has been done, what the purpose of a message is, what a table shows.
- Traps: options reuse words from the text but change the meaning: a limit becomes an obligation, "can" becomes "must", a rule becomes its opposite; a detail is offered instead of the purpose; in a table the comparison is reversed (less / more, before / after, fastest / slowest).
- DOK: about 2 at Level 1 (paraphrase of one sentence) and 3 at Level 2 (purpose, reason, comparing numbers in a table).

### Part 2 (questions 6 to 11, 6 marks): choose the best reply
- Printed: one line said by a person (in quotation marks, 6 to 20 words: an offer, a question, a worry, a piece of news), and three possible replies A B C. Students circle the best reply. Example: an invitation with "Yes, I'd love to!" as the answer.
- What it tests: the grammar AND the function together. The right reply matches the question type and its time: thanking for an offer with an adverb of indefinite time, saying what you would like to do (verb + infinitive), saying what something is for (infinitive of purpose), answering a worry with a modal of possibility, comparing with a comparative adverb, giving advice with an imperative.
- Traps: the wrong replies are on the same topic but answer a different question (where or when instead of why or what for), are about the past when the question is about the future, use a time word that does not make sense, or reverse a comparison.
- DOK: about 2 at Level 1 and 4 at Level 2.

### Part 3 (questions 12 to 17, 6 marks): circle the correct word
- Printed: six separate sentences (6 to 14 words), each with one gap, and three words or short phrases printed under it. Students circle one. Example: a quantifier before a plural noun.
- What it tests: two tag questions (one after a positive sentence, one after a negative sentence), a quantifier with an uncountable noun, an indefinite pronoun, a time connective linking two instructions, a verb followed by -ing or to + verb.
- Traps: the wrong auxiliary or the wrong tense in the tag, the wrong polarity, a countable quantifier with an uncountable noun, "anything" in a positive sentence, the base form or the wrong pattern after a verb.
- DOK: all 6 at Level 1.

### Part 4 (questions 18 to 24, 7 marks): text with gaps, three options for each gap
- Printed: a short recount text with a title (about 180 words, 5 or 6 short paragraphs) written by a child about something their class did, with an example gap (0) and seven numbered gaps. Under the text, a table: for each number, three options. Students circle one.
- What it tests (one objective per gap, all different): a quantifier, infinitive of purpose, a time connective, a modal (possibility vs obligation), a comparative adverb, an indefinite pronoun, an adverb of indefinite time with the present perfect. The example gap is also a quantifier.
- Traps: three forms of the same word (base form / -ing / to + verb; adverb / comparative / superlative), a word that fits the grammar but not the meaning of the text (students must read the sentence before or after), an uncountable quantifier with a plural noun.
- DOK: about 5 at Level 1, 2 at Level 2 (where the sentence before or after decides the answer).

### Part 5 (questions 25 to 29, 5 marks): three people, who said it?
- Printed: three short first-person texts by three children (about 65 words each), each with a drawn portrait and a name, all on one unit theme. Then five questions "Who ...?", each with the three names as options A B C.
- Questions paraphrase the texts and ask about feelings, opinions and reasons: who thinks one thing matters more than another, who enjoys something about where they are, who has to be careful sometimes, who would like a change, who has something that helps them.
- Traps: each text contains words that seem to match more than one question; the right child is the one whose MEANING matches, not whose words match. One name is the answer two or three times; every name is used at least once.
- DOK: all 5 at Level 2.

### Part 6 (questions 30 to 34, 5 marks): an article, three options
- Printed: a third-person article about an event (about 200 words, 6 short paragraphs) with a title and a line drawing. It includes one quotation from a child. Five questions in text order, each with three options A B C (sentences of 5 to 12 words).
- Question styles: the main purpose; why something happened (a reason); how someone felt or what surprised them; what we can understand about one part (inference); what is most likely to happen next (prediction).
- Traps: options that use topic words but are not in the text, the reverse of what happened, and a detail that is close to the right answer but is not what the question asks.
- DOK: about 1 at Level 1 and 4 at Level 2.

## Paper 3: Writing (35 minutes, 20 marks, 2 tasks)
Both tasks have the same shape: a title in bold, one line of prompt, "Write 6 to 8 sentences ...", four bullet points beginning "In your writing:", and the reminder "Use descriptive adjectives and connectives to organise your ideas (into a paragraph)." Students write on lines. 6 to 8 short sentences is about 40 to 70 words, which matches the specification's 40 to 70 word target for Grade 3.

- Task 1 (10 marks): describe a PERSON. The four bullets ask: who the person is; what they are like; what they do; why they matter to you (opinion with a reason).
- Task 2 (10 marks): describe a PLACE or THING linked to a unit topic. The four bullets ask: what it is and where; what it looks like; one special feature and what it is used for (infinitive of purpose); why you like it (opinion with a reason).
- The two real prompts are RESERVED (see `stops-brief.md`): practise with other people and other places.

Mark scheme (from the school's answer key; NOT scaled from Grade 5). Each task has five categories, 0, 1 or 2 points each, total 10:

| Category | LOs | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task achievement and content | 4Wc.02, 4Wc.03 | all four bullets covered clearly, with a developed reason | some bullet details missing, unclear or thin | mostly off-topic or very little |
| Organisation and layout | 4Wor.02, 4Wca.03 | one clear paragraph, logical order, connectives link the sentences | some order, connectives limited or repeated | no logical order, disconnected sentences |
| Grammar range and accuracy | 4Wca.04 | mostly accurate sentences (present simple; for Task 2 also descriptive structures and infinitive of purpose), some variety, minor errors | basic structures, several errors, meaning clear | frequent errors stop meaning |
| Vocabulary and spelling | 4Wc.02, 4Wca.02 | varied, suitable words for the topic; familiar words spelt right | basic or repeated words, several spelling errors | very limited words, spelling blocks meaning |
| Writing conventions | 4Wca.03, 4Wca.04 | complete sentences, capital letters and full stops mostly right, legible | some incomplete sentences or punctuation errors | frequent sentence-boundary and punctuation errors |

Writing DOK (specification): at least 20% Level 1, 30% Level 2, 40% Level 3 across the writing marks (describing is Level 2; giving and developing a reason is Level 3).

## Item-writing rules
1. Three options in every multiple-choice question (Listening Parts 1 and 2; Reading Parts 1 to 6). One is clearly right. The other two must be tempting but clearly wrong for a careful reader or listener. In Reading Part 5 the three options are always the three people's names.
2. Options should be about the same length and the same type (three pictures of rooms, three times, three short sentences, three forms of one word). Do not make the right answer the longest.
3. Listening: every wrong option must be heard or seen in the talk. Use the real traps: "No, ...", "usually ... but today ...", a changed mind, a friend's wrong guess, a third person's choice, last year vs this year.
4. Listening Part 3 style gaps: answers are one word, a number, or a number + one word. At least one answer per form is a word the speaker spells letter by letter, written as letters with hyphens in the script (R-O-S-S); it must be spelt exactly. Give numbers digit by digit for phone numbers.
5. Keep sentences short (most under 12 words) and the language at Stage 4. Use only the grammar in the objectives list above plus present simple, present continuous, past simple, going to, will and present perfect with already / yet / never / ever.
6. Every wrong option gets a short, kind "why" (one sentence) that tells the child what to check.
7. Write the "worked" line as a teacher would explain the answer in one or two short sentences, quoting the key words of the text or talk.
8. Hints point at the strategy, never give the answer ("Listen to what she says AFTER 'No'.", "Is the noun countable or uncountable?").
9. Spread the right answer position (A, B, C) evenly.
10. No trick questions, no answer that depends on world knowledge outside the text.
11. Never copy sentences from any real exam paper or published book, and never use a reserved situation.
